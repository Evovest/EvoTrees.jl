Decorrelation penalty: design ​

This page describes how ctrl_lambda is defined and computed. For usage, see the decorrelation tutorial.

Notation: n training rows with weights w_i and total weight W; p the raw predictions (on the link scale); controls c_1, ..., c_k, each centred and scaled to unit standard deviation; λ = ctrl_lambda and ω_j the entries of ctrl_weights (1 by default).

Statistic ​

Dependence is measured by the unbiased squared distance covariance dcov2(p, c) (Székely and Rizzo, 2014), which is zero in population only under independence. It is the same for p and -p, so the penalty pushes towards independence rather than a negative correlation.

Pearson correlation is cheaper (O(n)) but only captures linear dependence. Distance covariance also captures nonlinear dependence, at a cost of O(n log n). Distance correlation costs the same, but its gradient is inversely proportional to the spread of p, so it pushes hardest when the predictions are nearly flat. The gradient of dcov2 depends on p only through its order, so its push is steady.

The estimator is a U-statistic and is not floored at zero, so near independence it is negative about half the time. It needs at least 4 rows. A control with at most one value either side of a tie has zero distance variance and is rejected, or skipped within a group.

We considered two alternatives: clipping negative estimates at zero and using the V-statistic. Clipping caused groups to alternate between active and inactive penalties, while the V-statistic shrank predictions and reduced their correlation with the target. We therefore retain the unbiased estimator. Very large weights still inflate the predictions (see Step size below), so tune the weight on held-out data.

Objective ​

Under :mse the training objective is

sum_i w_i (y_i - p_i)^2 + λ W sum_j ω_j dcov2(p, c_j)

The factor W puts the penalty on the scale of the summed loss, so λ depends neither on n nor on the scale of the weights, and the standardised controls make it independent of their units. It does scale with the target: dcov2 is homogeneous of degree one in p and the squared error of degree two, so a target 10 times larger needs 10 times the weight.

dcov2 is piecewise linear in p, so the penalty adds λ W ∂dcov2/∂p_i to the gradient of row i and nothing to the Hessian.

The penalty acts on the full prediction, offset included, in training and in evaluation. Under :mse, fitting y - offset without an offset penalises the trees' part alone.

Curvature weighting ​

Under other losses the penalty gradient of row i is multiplied by h_i / (2 w_i), the loss's Hessian relative to that of :mse, so a given λ moves leaves about as much as under :mse. Without it the penalty dominates rows where the likelihood is flat, such as :logloss rows predicted near 0 or 1, and the fit diverges.

Since the gradient g of dcov2(p, c) depends on p only through its order, g_i φ'(p_i) is the gradient of dcov2(φ(p), c) for any increasing φ, and each loss's curvature is such a derivative. The penalty term per control, in the units of the loss's own metric, is therefore:

LossPenalty termExact
:mseλ dcov2(p, c)yes
:loglossλ / 2 * dcov2(sigmoid(p), c)yes
:poissonλ dcov2(exp(p), c)yes
:gammaλ dcov2(p, c)at a calibrated fit
:tweedieλ / (2 - ρ) * dcov2(exp((2 - ρ) p), c), ρ = 1.5at a calibrated fit
:gaussian_mleλ / 2 * mean(σ^-2) * dcov2(μ, c)at a constant scale σ

Under :gaussian_mle only the location μ is penalised. In the code φ is _dep_transform and the coefficient _dep_coef.

Pooled and within groups ​

By default one dcov2 is computed over all rows. With ctrl_within_group = true, group g of group_name / group_train adds λ W_g dcov2(p_g, c_g), with W_g the total training weight of its rows (its number of rows without weights) and the control standardised within the group. Under :mse the objective is

sum_i w_i (y_i - p_i)^2 + λ sum_j ω_j sum_g W_g dcov2(p_g, c_jg)

with p_g and c_jg the predictions and control j on the rows of group g. Each group's penalty carries the weight its rows carry in the loss, and dcov2 is unweighted within a group. Groups with fewer than 4 rows, or whose control has no usable spread, are skipped.

Only the dependence within groups counts, not differences in the control's level or spread between groups. Under :mse the penalty gradient sums to zero within each group, since dcov2 does not change when a constant is added to p_g. The trees can still move group means through features that vary between groups, and the loss corrects those moves. Groups run in parallel.

Several controls ​

With several controls the penalty is sum_j ω_j dcov2(p, c_j), not the distance covariance of p with the vector of controls: the O(n log n) algorithm below holds for two univariate variables only, and the sum makes each control's weight explicit. A dependence visible only jointly is not penalised, for example p = sign(c_1) sign(c_2) with independent, symmetric controls. Pooled controls run in parallel.

Algorithm ​

With a_ij = |p_i - p_j|, b_ij = |c_i - c_j|, row sums a_i., b_i. and totals a.., b..:

dcov2 = (S1 - 2 S2 / (n - 2) + a.. b.. / ((n - 1)(n - 2))) / (n (n - 3))
S1 = sum_ij a_ij b_ij,   S2 = sum_i a_i. b_i.

Row sums come from a sort and prefix sums. S1 comes from a sweep over the rows in the order of p, with Fenwick trees indexed by the rank of c holding the counts and sums of the rows already visited (Chaudhuri and Hu, 2019). For the gradient, ∂S1/∂p_i = 2 sum_j sign(p_i - p_j) b_ij takes a forward and a backward sweep, and the other terms need only the number of rows below and above p_i and their b sums. Per control and round this is one sort of p and two sweeps: O(n log n) time and O(n) memory instead of O(n²). The control's row sums and ranks are computed once per fit.

Tied predictions, common in boosting, count on neither side (the symmetric subgradient of |p_i - p_j| at zero), so the gradient does not depend on row order. Since dcov2 is homogeneous of degree one and g depends only on the order of p, dcov2(p, c) = sum_i g_i p_i, which gives the value from the gradient.

Controls and the penalty's sums are held in Float64, since in Float32 a control on a large scale loses its resolution (the step is 128 at a Unix timestamp). Sums run in index order from a shifted origin, so fits agree across CPUs and keep their precision at large values.

At a million rows on 8 threads, one pooled control took about 0.56 s per round (its sweeps are sequential), against 0.06 s to grow the tree. Within 1000 groups of 1000 rows, each control took about 0.025 s per round.

Evaluation metric ​

With evaluation data, λ > 0 and the loss's own metric (the default), the logged metric adds the penalty term of the table above, so early stopping follows the penalised objective. The term is computed on the evaluation set with its controls standardised there (within each evaluation group under ctrl_within_group, each group's term weighted by W_g / W from the evaluation weights). Under :mse the metric is then the training objective on the evaluation set divided by its total weight. For a maximised metric such as :gaussian_mle the term is subtracted. Other metrics have no exchange rate with λ and are reported unchanged.

The logger also keeps :base_metrics, the metric without the penalty, and :ctrl_dependence, each control's dcov2(φ(p), c_j) before λ and the weights. The callback adds what eval_penalty! returns and holds no loss-specific code.

GPU ​

The sort and the sweeps do not map onto a per-row kernel, so on GPU the penalty runs on the host. Each round the penalised gradient rows, their Hessian rows, the weights and the predictions are copied to the host, and the penalised gradient rows back. At a million rows on an A10G this took about 0.34 s per round pooled and 0.026 s within 1000 groups, against 0.011 s for the trees.

Step size ​

The penalty adds nothing to the Hessian and its gradient depends on the order of p, not its spread, so its step does not shrink as the predictions flatten. Past some λ * eta, each round overshoots independence and the fit oscillates instead of settling. In synthetic runs with predictions of standard deviation about 1, this started at a λ * eta between 3 and 10 pooled and at 30 or more within groups, with the correlation to the control swinging by up to ±0.3 under :mse and ±0.8 under :logloss. A larger weight needs a smaller eta.

Without an offset the first round carries no penalty, since tied predictions give a zero gradient. The second round applies the full push to nearly flat predictions and can overshoot to a negative correlation, which later rounds correct.

At very large weights the predictions inflate, through the oscillation and through negative estimates: once dcov2 is below zero, scaling the predictions up lowers the penalty. Within groups at λ = 10000 and eta = 0.01, the per-group standard deviation of the predictions grew from 0.97 without a penalty to 13.9.
