Pearson loss: design ​

This page describes how loss = :pearson is defined and computed. For usage, see the Pearson loss tutorial.

Notation: for a group g with n_g rows i, weights w_i, total weight W_g and a_i = w_i / W_g; p the predictions and p̄_g = sum_i a_i p_i; y the target, standardised within the group as ỹ_i = (y_i - ȳ_g) / s_g, with ȳ_g and s_g its weighted mean and standard deviation.

Objective ​

The correlation itself is a poor Newton objective: its Hessian scales with the correlation, so it is near zero at low correlations. Instead, each group's centred prediction is fitted to its standardised target by weighted squared error, as in CatBoost's QueryRMSE:

L = sum_g sum_i w_i (p_i - p̄_g - ỹ_i)^2
g_i = 2 w_i (p_i - p̄_g - ỹ_i)
h_i = 2 w_i

The gradient is exact, since sum_i w_i (p_i - p̄_g - ỹ_i) = 0 within each group. h_i is the exact diagonal without the centring term -2 w_i^2 / W_g, so it bounds the curvature from above.

With s the weighted standard deviation of p within the group and r its weighted correlation with y,

sum_i a_i (p_i - p̄_g - ỹ_i)^2 = (s - r)^2 + 1 - r^2

For a given direction of the predictions, a group's term is smallest at s = r when r > 0, where it equals 1 - r^2, so lowering the loss raises the correlation.

Groups and weights ​

Each group's term weighs by its total weight W_g, so with unit weights a group counts in proportion to its size. Weights w_i = 1 / n_g make every group count the same. A group with fewer than two rows or a constant target has g_i = h_i = 0, and fitting fails if no group is left.

A constant added to a group's predictions changes neither the loss nor the correlation, so no bias is fitted.
